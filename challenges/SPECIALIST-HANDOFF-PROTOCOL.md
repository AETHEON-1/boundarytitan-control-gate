# Specialist Handoff Protocol

## Purpose

BoundaryTitan does not treat public silence as validation, rejection, consent, or closure.

Tagging a specialist, posting publicly, or publishing a challenge creates an invitation. It does not create evidence.

The purpose of this protocol is to convert broad public invitations into bounded specialist handoffs focused on one consequential arrow at a time.

## Core rule

Do not ask a specialist to review the whole framework when one disputed arrow can be isolated.

A valid handoff should present:

1. the frozen claim;
2. the established evidence;
3. the disputed transition `A -> B`;
4. the first unsupported arrow, if known;
5. the hidden or required premise under examination;
6. the consequence if the arrow is accepted;
7. the consequence owner;
8. the external stop authority;
9. the falsification condition;
10. one bounded specialist question.

## Specialist question form

Preferred form:

> What fact, law, theorem, standard, domain constraint, or counterexample makes this arrow valid, invalid, or underdetermined?

The specialist is not asked to endorse BoundaryTitan.

The specialist is asked to attack a bounded claim inside a domain they can inspect.

## Credentials boundary

Credentials may help route a challenge to a relevant person.

Credentials do not establish the truth of the claim.

Job title, institution, seniority, reputation, degree, follower count, publication count, or status may not substitute for evidence supporting an arrow.

Identity establishes attribution. It does not establish truth.

## Silence boundary

`NO RESPONSE != SUPPORT`

`NO RESPONSE != REJECTION`

`NO RESPONSE != CONSENT`

`NO RESPONSE != SPECIALIST REVIEW`

A failed handoff attempt records only that no attributable evidence was received through that route.

Repeated silence may justify changing the routing strategy. It may not be converted into a favorable disposition.

## Allowed handoff states

- `PREPARED`
- `SENT`
- `ACKNOWLEDGED`
- `EVIDENCE RECEIVED`
- `DECLINED`
- `NO RESPONSE`
- `ROUTING FAILED`
- `CLOSED BY HUMAN DISPOSITION`

These states describe the handoff process only. They do not determine the truth or authority state of the underlying challenge.

## Evidence handling

Incoming specialist responses are evidence inputs, not closure.

For every response, preserve:

- exact attribution;
- date;
- source location;
- challenged arrow;
- factual claims supplied;
- cited evidence;
- counterexamples;
- assumptions;
- contradictions;
- unresolved questions;
- whether the response addresses epistemic support, authority, consequence ownership, or stop power.

Do not collapse disagreement between specialists into artificial consensus.

## Routing strategy

Prefer the narrowest competent route.

Examples:

- cryptographic key custody -> cryptography / IAM / security engineering;
- legal authority -> relevant jurisdiction / statutory or case-law expertise;
- medical standard of care -> relevant medical specialty and governing evidence base;
- market structure -> market microstructure / securities regulation / economics as applicable;
- newsroom editorial authority -> journalism ethics / newsroom governance / media law;
- infrastructure stop path -> systems engineering / SRE / safety engineering / operations.

A challenge may require multiple disciplines when the arrow crosses a domain boundary.

## Machine boundary

The repository may prepare handoffs, classify responses, preserve evidence, surface contradictions, and identify unresolved arrows.

It may not:

- impersonate specialist assent;
- infer validation from silence;
- certify that specialist review is complete;
- determine final truth or authority state from its own output;
- close a challenge without attributable human disposition where such disposition is required.

Repository state is not permission state.

CAN does not imply MAY.

## Final brake

Final specialist disposition remains outside the machine-generated evidence path.

No owner, no brake.

No ledger, no trust.

Only Green proceeds.
