# A Carpenter’s Note

I did not begin this work because I believe machines are magic.

I began because I kept seeing the same mistake: people confuse what a system can do with what the system is allowed to do. Then they confuse what it is allowed to do with what it is authorized to do. Then the consequence arrives, and everyone looks for the missing owner.

I know how a physical system behaves. A wall can look straight and still be carrying a hidden weakness. A tool can work perfectly and still be the wrong tool for the job. A house can look immaculate until the weather finds the joint everyone stopped looking at.

That is the attitude behind this repository.

I am not trying to stop capability. Capability is useful. I am trying to stop harmful transitions of unearned authority.

```text
Capability
→ access
→ permission
→ authority
→ action
→ consequence
```

The missing arrows matter more than the impressive parts.

The system may be able to produce an answer, a recommendation, a design, a route, a classification, a robot motion, or a new assistant. None of those facts, by themselves, tell me that the thing should happen, that it is authorized, that it is safe, or that anyone has agreed to carry the consequence.

That is why I keep returning to a few simple questions:

- What changed?
- What supports it?
- What remains unknown?
- Who owns what happens next?
- Who can still say no?

The machine can help ask those questions. It can inspect, compare, challenge, preserve, and route. It can find what humans threw away. It can take a Sledgehammer to the assumptions.

But the machine cannot be its own verification. It cannot grant itself new permission. It cannot clear its own freeze. It cannot become the final judge of the machine.

I am also not claiming that this repository is complete. It is deliberately open to attack. The name is The Impossible System because the work refuses to pretend that the boundary is finished.

If the architecture is wrong, find the counterexample.

If the language hides an authority transition, expose it.

If a lane is bleeding into another lane, show where.

If the brake can be bypassed, it was never a real brake.

If a weakness remains, preserve it and build around it.

I am giving this work away because I cannot be the final judge of me. The repository cannot be the final judge of the repository. Specialists must be able to inspect it, challenge it, narrow it, correct it, or reject it.

The point is not to make the machine less capable.

The point is to make the human harder to bypass.

> **No owner, no brake. No ledger, no trust.**

— The Carpenter of Impossible Systems
