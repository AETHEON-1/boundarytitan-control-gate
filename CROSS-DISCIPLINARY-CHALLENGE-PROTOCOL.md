# Cross-Disciplinary Challenge Protocol

## Purpose

Any specialist, practitioner, researcher, regulator, engineer, lawyer, economist, journalist, hacker, tradesperson, or informed outsider may submit a consequential claim without relying on credentials. The submission is evaluated by its evidence path and inferential structure.

The protocol attacks the handoff between disciplines, not the disciplines themselves.

CAN ⊬ MAY.

A successful defense of A does not establish A → B.

## Core Rules

1. Attack the arrow, not the discipline.
2. Freeze the exact claim before debating it.
3. Separate evidence from authority.
4. Locate the first unsupported arrow.
5. Preserve uncertainty where evidence ends.
6. Every consequential transition requires a named authority source.
7. Name the consequence owner.
8. Identify who can still say no.
9. Keep final stop authority external to the system being tested.
10. No self-certification.
11. No credential substitutes for evidence.
12. BoundaryTitan claims receive no privileged status.

## Credential Boundary

Contributor identity may establish attribution.

Contributor identity may not establish truth.

Credentials may identify a relevant specialist for review.

Credentials may not substitute for evidence supporting the challenged arrow.

No challenge record contains degree, institution, job title, or years-of-experience fields. Optional domain context may be provided for routing, but never as epistemic weight.

## Evidence States

Allowed evidence states:

- SUPPORTED
- CONTRADICTED
- INDETERMINATE
- NOT TESTED

These states describe evidence only. They do not authorize action, deployment, publication, certification, acceptance, or closure.

## Allowed Dispositions

The protocol returns dispositions, not winners.

- DERIVED
- REQUIRES PREMISE
- SUPPORTED
- CONTRADICTED
- UNDERDETERMINED
- AUTHORITY UNESTABLISHED
- CONSEQUENCE OWNER MISSING
- STOP AUTHORITY MISSING

The protocol must not produce SPECIALIST WON, BOUNDARYTITAN WON, SAFE, CERTIFIED, APPROVED, or equivalent self-closing labels.

## The Arrow Challenge

Choose any consequence-bearing system.

Find one transition:

- Evidence → Conclusion
- Prediction → Decision
- Recommendation → Action
- Capability → Permission
- Policy → Enforcement
- Metric → Classification
- Classification → Treatment
- Risk Score → Denial
- Price Signal → Allocation
- Model Output → Human Consequence

Show that the arrow is justified.

Or break it.

No credential wins the argument.

No outsider loses by default.

The evidence path must survive.

## Self-Application Rule

BoundaryTitan claims receive no privileged status.

Any invariant, rule, inference, schema, control assumption, or authority claim in this repository may be challenged using the same record.

Nothing gets immunity because we built it.

## Authority Boundary

Repository state ≠ permission state.

Machine output cannot authorize, certify, or close itself.

The protocol may structure, compare, route, and preserve challenge evidence. It may not decide who is legitimate, declare a consequential action authorized, accept residual risk, or extinguish external stop authority.

Only Green proceeds.

No owner, no brake.

No ledger, no trust.
