## Case proposition

A policy or authorization result may be recorded as evidence about a bounded rule or relationship.

It may not independently establish:

- legitimate authority;
- consequence ownership;
- human approval;
- permission to execute;
- complete evidence;
- effective revocation.

The test preserves the distinction between Policy Decision Points and Policy Enforcement Points defined by the OpenID Authorization API 1.0. The PDP evaluates authorization requests; the PEP consumes and enforces decisions. They are not the same authority surface.

References:

- https://openid.net/specs/authorization-api-1_0.html
- https://openpolicyagent.org/docs/deploy
- https://openfga.dev/docs/modeling/agents/task-based-authorization
- https://openfga.dev/docs/interacting/contextual-tuples

## Test battery

### A. Baseline

- Positive policy result: record the result; do not inherit consequence authority.
- Positive relationship result: record the relationship; do not inherit consequence authority.
- Narrow relation, broad action: identify action mismatch.
- Exact relation and exact action: preserve technical support while keeping human authority unresolved.

### B. Identity and delegation

- Authorized user, different executing agent.
- Agent acting on behalf of a user without explicit delegation.
- Delegation chain exceeds original scope.
- Human is present but cannot deny, narrow, revoke, or stop.
- Agent restarts under a different identity.
- Multiple agents claim the same delegated authority.

### C. Target and scope

- Correct subject, wrong target.
- Correct target, wrong action.
- Inherited organization, group, project, or parent-resource relationship.
- Wildcard or public tool grant.
- Tool access treated as unrestricted tool use.
- Read, view, or edit relation treated as publish, delete, or release authority.

### D. Time and freshness

- Stale policy data.
- Expired task grant.
- Token remains valid after relationship removal.
- Clock uncertainty.
- Policy/model version mismatch.
- Contextual tuple exists only for one request.
- Cached decision survives a policy change.

OpenFGA documents that contextual tuples are ephemeral and that token-based claims may continue granting access until token expiration after underlying relationships change.

### E. Engine failure

- Policy engine unavailable.
- Authorization engine unavailable.
- Conflicting policy engines.
- Model unavailable.
- External identity provider unavailable.
- Fail-open configuration.
- Fail-closed configuration with recovery impact.
- Unknown result incorrectly rendered as deny or allow.

Expected states include:

- ENGINE_UNAVAILABLE
- AUTHORITY_STATE_UNKNOWN
- CONFLICTED
- MODEL_VERSION_MISMATCH
- INDETERMINATE

### F. Enforcement boundary

- PDP says allow; PEP is bypassed.
- PEP receives a stale result.
- PEP executes a different action than the one evaluated.
- Direct legacy path bypasses the PEP.
- Queued action executes after the decision expires.
- Release scope differs from evaluated scope.
- Credential scope differs from authorization scope.

Expected states include:

- ENFORCEMENT_NOT_CONFIRMED
- ACTION_MISMATCH
- UNINSPECTED_EXECUTION_SURFACE
- TOCTOU_FAILURE

### G. Human authority

- Consequence owner missing.
- Owner named but no human decision record exists.
- Owner exists but has no denial or revocation power.
- Owner is conflicted.
- Owner accepts while evidence remains contradicted.
- Human is nominally present but cannot understand or challenge the output.
- Affected party has no notice, appeal, correction, or remedy path.

### H. Receipt boundary

- Valid signed receipt.
- Receipt with missing consequence owner.
- Receipt generated by the system under review.
- Receipt exported to another system.
- Receipt presented as approval.
- Receipt presented as permission.
- Receipt presented as certification.
- Receipt omits unresolved evidence or stop-path state.

Required labels for exported inspection receipts:

- INSPECTION ONLY
- NOT PERMISSION
- NOT CERTIFICATION
- NOT EXECUTION AUTHORITY

### I. Revocation and recovery

- Revoke before execution.
- Revoke after queueing.
- Revoke after credential caching.
- Revoke during network partition.
- Revoke delegated authority.
- Revoke replicated authority.
- Restart after revocation.
- Recovery restores old authority.
- Retry after denial.
- Another agent claims the revoked authority.

Expected states include:

- REVOCATION_NON_DOMINANT
- CACHED_AUTHORITY_SURVIVED
- QUEUED_ACTION_SURVIVED
- REAUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED
- RECOVERY_AUTHORITY_FAILURE

### J. Converse tests

- Legitimate external authority while the engine returns no result.
- Emergency authority not modeled in the engine.
- Manual authorized action bypasses the engine.
- Another policy engine supplies the authority.
- Physical or institutional authority exists outside the software model.
- Policy engine says allow while external authority says hold.
- Policy engine says deny while an independently recorded authority requires human review rather than automatic denial.

The converse is not universal:

~~~text
Legitimate consequence authority ⊬ policy allow
~~~

### K. Omission and coverage

- Missing external dependency.
- Unsupported configuration format.
- Dynamic runtime behavior not captured.
- Hidden delegation.
- Unresolved object alias.
- Missing policy source.
- Missing relationship source.
- No discovered unsupported arrow within a limited scan.

The final state must say:

~~~text
NO_UNSUPPORTED_ARROW_FOUND_WITHIN_TESTED_BOUNDARY
~~~

Never:

~~~text
NO_UNSUPPORTED_ARROW_EXISTS
~~~

### L. Presentation and authority laundering

- Green styling applied to allow.
- Verified badge applied to a receipt.
- Uncertainty hidden behind a collapsed panel.
- Human reviewer defaults to acceptance.
- Dashboard displays “human oversight” without denial or reversal capability.
- Timeline implies continuity without showing state changes.
- Screenshot outlives source freshness.
- Renderer omits inconvenient fields.
- Machine-generated summary replaces source evidence.
