## 4. Proposed shared-dependency investigation

**Hypothesis only:** organizationally separate authorities can lose operational independence when their enforcement or recovery paths require a shared failure-prone dependency. Do not infer an actual common-mode failure from supplier overlap, consolidation or market concentration alone.

**Bounded test design:** define system, specific prohibited consequence, independent gate, named owner, material dependencies, alternate path and intervention deadline. Map identities, credentials, administrative controls, network/control planes, update sources, hosting, legal and contractual dependencies, recovery resources and evidence custody. Enumerate common cut sets for that scoped graph and compare to independently discovered paths. In a separately authorized laboratory, isolate each candidate dependency; record attempted transitions, independent stop authority, downstream commit status, surviving privileges, recovery timing and retained evidence. The proposition is defeated for a defined dependency/path if independent stop and recovery demonstrably remain effective despite its loss/compromise. Test design is candidate only; no execution or completeness finding.

**Inverse/converse controls:** shared supplier does not entail shared failure; distinct suppliers do not entail independent dependencies; technical independence does not establish legitimate jurisdiction; successful recovery does not establish complete control. Denial, containment and extinction are separate states. Preserve counterexamples, alternative controls, negative results and unidentified paths.

**Omissions carried forward:** financing and narrative funding provenance; counterparty exposures and overlapping guarantees; independent shutdown across asynchronous execution; provider-independent recovery; citizen contestability and remedies. Asia, Africa, Latin America and other regions not systematically sampled. No broad global coverage claim.
