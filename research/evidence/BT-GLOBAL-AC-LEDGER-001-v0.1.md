## Scope and non-claims

This is a **purposive, non-exhaustive sample of seven decision/authorization boundaries** raised in the November 23 global scan and omissions pass. It is not an inventory of all global incidents, actors, suppliers, jurisdictions, finances, consequences, approvals, or paths. A negative outcome (no consensus) and a laboratory authorization are deliberately included as controls and explicitly marked as such. The ledger documents *which transition has evidence* and *where evidence stops*; it does not decide whether any decision was lawful, wise, or safe.

**No entry has an independently verified completeness boundary.** Every entry is therefore labeled `COVERAGE: PARTIAL / COMPLETENESS UNVERIFIED`, even when the narrow fact of a decision is corroborated. The absence of public evidence is **UNKNOWN**, never proof of absence. Source links and quotations below are retrieval pointers, not custody-grade archived copies, verified content hashes, or formal independent audits.

### Evidence key

- **P:** Actor-authored primary record (press release, filing, vendor notice, or researcher report). Establishes the existence of the claim, not its independent truth.
- **R:** Independently maintained public record of filing or law (e.g. SEC EDGAR, chaptered legislative text). Establishes filing/enactment record, not an external validation of the merits, risk models, or exact execution paths.
- **I:** Reporting or observation independent of the decision-maker. Can corroborate a bounded event or reproduce a bounded effect, but is not necessarily an independently controlled full-path audit.
- **V:** Independent technical or institutional review of the disputed claim, with defined scope, evidence custody, and reproducibility. **None retrieved sufficient for full-path authorization, stop, and recovery closure.**

**Result vocabulary:** `OBSERVED`, `REPORTED`, `INFERRED`, `UNKNOWN`, `NOT TESTED`, `NOT APPLICABLE`, `COVERAGE PARTIAL`. The first unsupported arrow is the exact point beyond which no conclusion may be promoted.

## Cross-domain dependency and composition notes

1. **Corporate decision × public authority:** AC-01 board action and government assurances are different jurisdictional acts; a risk assurance does not transfer every investigatory or enforcement role to the board.
2. **Finance × infrastructure:** AC-02 requires actual payment, power, construction, supply and counterparty delivery paths before systemic-risk coverage can be claimed.
3. **Credential × permission × consequence:** AC-04 and AC-05 expose different possible bridges where a technically accepted capability may be exercised for an unintended purpose. Their proof strength and mechanisms are not interchangeable.
4. **Stop × extinction:** AC-03 separates a blocked new attack path from cleaning devices; AC-04/05 lack verified retroactive recovery; AC-06 remains future institutional machinery rather than an observed technical brake.
5. **Distributed authority × no consensus:** AC-07 is a negative control: the absence of collective authorization must not be described as a single sovereign's permission or as cancellation of earlier obligations.

**Cross-case hypothesis only:** local authorization/permission may fail to establish globally justified downstream consequences. A shared *question* does not establish a shared failure mechanism, universal theorem, or tested independent enforcement mechanism.
