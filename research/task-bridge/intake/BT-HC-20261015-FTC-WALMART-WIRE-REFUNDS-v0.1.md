## HTK / look at this
A government agency can send a refund check without establishing that the intended person received or cashed it. In the Walmart wire-transfer fraud case, the FTC announced 65,022 checks totaling more than $9.8 million to eligible consumers. This is a concrete remedy process, not proof of complete victim recovery or permanent shutdown of scam money rails.

## Observed / inferred / unknown
- FTC announced $10 million settlement judgment in June 2025, with order restrictions on processing fraud-induced money transfers. Settlement of allegations is not a trial finding of every allegation.
- FTC announced >$9.8 million in 65,022 checks sent in October 2026 for eligible transfers at Walmart from Jan 2015-Dec 2024; recipients instructed to cash checks within 90 days.
- The case allegation period and refund eligibility window differ; do not collapse them.
- The FTC says it never requires payment or account information to obtain the refund.
- Inference: the 90-day cashing window creates a measurable conversion from issued remedy to realized funds; replacement/reissuance processes may matter, but are not established here.
- Not independently verified: check delivery, cashing, actual funds received, total eligible victims, average loss recovered, refunds to people outside selection, ongoing compliance with injunction, re-entry through other rails, or case-specific scam extinction.
- Rival explanation: lower realized redemption may reflect address problems, lost checks, deliberate non-cashing, or delayed processing, not solely institutional refusal.
- Contradiction/negative: 65,022 checks issued is not 65,022 confirmed cash receipts; $9.8m announced is not $9.8m confirmed paid. Do not infer the opposite (no one paid).

External stop: court-enforceable order and provider-level transaction denial, with actual effectiveness untested. Other processors are outside this order.
Reversibility: monetary redress may partly offset past loss; money already sent to scammers and later harm are not automatically reversible.
Residual risk: incomplete restitution, scam migration, inaccessible redress, ongoing transfer exposure.
