## Claim / first unsupported arrow / contrary evidence
**Claim under test:** early large-load interconnection can be made reliable and non-cost-shifting through enforceable interim flexibility before grid upgrades.
**First unsupported arrow:** CAISO board approval -> FERC-effective tariff and utility contract -> independently executable remote disconnect -> measured timely load reduction -> reliable service and non-shifted costs for other customers.
**Observed:** CAISO has approved a proposal, not demonstrated any particular emergency curtailment; transmission owners are expected to develop site-specific contracts and tariff financing provisions. Public aggregate upgrade/cost estimates are proposed, not verified actual customer bills.
**Counterevidence / limitations:** Board memo acknowledges scope gaps for below-threshold and distribution-connected loads, concerns raised by PG&E and Cal Advocates over jurisdiction and allocation, and future work on permanently flexible loads. The planned FERC filing is a future action, not approval. A 50 MW threshold does not mean smaller loads have no transmission effects.
**Rival explanations:** an independently operated utility disconnect could enforce a contract, or site-specific load flexibility may be economically and physically sufficient; neither can be inferred from a policy statement alone. Costs might be correctly allocated even if curtailment is never invoked; cost allocation and physical stop are separate propositions.
