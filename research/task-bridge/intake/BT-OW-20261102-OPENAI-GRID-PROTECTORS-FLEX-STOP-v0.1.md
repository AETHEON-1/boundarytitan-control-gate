## Claim / observations / missing evidence
- **Observed:** On 2026-11-02 OpenAI announced a national **prospective** Grid Protectors Program, promising utility-negotiated flexible load at future campuses, on-site generation/storage where appropriate, and campus cost responsibility. The text says local reduction quantity, timing and duration are to be agreed.
- **Observed:** The **Georgia** pilot has an earlier, utility-described 25-year contract and up-to-1,000-MW flexibility provision. Georgia Power says it may reduce energy delivered to the site during certain conditions. This is a stronger documented contractual control than the nationwide announcement.
- **Observed:** Georgia Power's $950m/year and $15/month numbers are **portfolio forecasts for 2029 onward**, not realized current savings and not independently audited attribution to the one OpenAI site.
- **Observed:** Georgia PSC's 2025 rule and utility's Aug 26 contract-approval announcement provide legal/regulatory context. The actual full signed contract, dispatch clauses, telemetry, penalties, independent cost accounting and regulator order were not inspected as complete authenticated records here.
- **Unknown:** Whether a live campus can ramp down within an emergency's required seconds/minutes; whether utility command has technical precedence over campus optimization; whether load reduction is measured at the interconnection point; what onsite generation does to fuel/emissions; how nonperformance penalties or collateral work; whether costs are shifted through generation/transmission upgrades, stranded assets, reserve requirements or changed demand forecasts; how household bills actually change.
- **Contrary possibility:** A properly enforced demand-response contract may meaningfully improve reliability and reduce cost for all ratepayers. The presence of untested details does NOT prove the agreement is ineffective or deceptive. Future campuses may legitimately differ in design and operator authority.
