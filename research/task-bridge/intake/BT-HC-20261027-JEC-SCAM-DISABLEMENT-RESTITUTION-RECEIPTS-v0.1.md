## Precise observed claims and limitations
- JEC reports **USSS sent more than 5,800 voluntary registrar takedown requests** from October 2024 through February 2026. **DHS did not provide the number of registrar compliances.** A request is not a domain suspension; neither establishes permanent denial or prevents re-registration. Report PDF pp. 2 and 8.
- FTC reported **nearly 700 consumer reports referred to the FBI Recovery Asset Team in 2024**, but did not provide a resolution breakdown. Referral is not a frozen wire or money returned. PDF p. 7.
- As of September 2026, DOJ's Scam Center Strike Force reportedly **restrained more than $930 million in cryptocurrency** from scam-compound-origin schemes. Restrained/frozen is not forfeited, distributed, or returned to identified victims. PDF pp. 10-11.
- FTC reported **$1.83 billion in FY 2025 consumer relief across fraud enforcement**, but FTC told the committee it **could not specifically identify which fraud matters and recovery amounts involved overseas scam compounds**. PDF pp. 2, 7-8.
- DHS reported **over $770 million returned through USSS asset seizures in FY 2024-2025**, explicitly across a range of fraud schemes, not limited to foreign scam compounds. FBI separately reported **more than $336 million returned to scam victims in 2025**. These periods, agencies and case populations differ; **do not sum or divide as a scam-compound restitution rate**. PDF pp. 8, 11.
- DOJ officials identified legal and administrative difficulty returning seized cryptocurrency, with victims often not made whole. This is attributed testimony, not an independently measured universal outcome.
- The report cites agency and media claims about successor compounds, infrastructure disruption and sanctions; it does not establish provider-specific technical stop receipts or complete campaign extinction.

## FIRST UNSUPPORTED ARROWS
**Registrar request -> identified domain actually disabled -> successor infrastructure denied -> campaign cannot reach victims.**
**Asset frozen -> legal custody and forfeiture -> eligible victims identified -> money disbursed -> victim receives usable funds.**

The first gap in the first chain is the absent **provider compliance denominator**. The first gap in the second is **custody/disposition of frozen funds**; disbursement and realized recovery remain later unproven arrows.

## Consequence-first backward trace
Possible continuing scam victim loss or unrepaired theft <- active replacement infrastructure or unrecovered funds <- domain/hosting, telecom/SIM, social accounts and payment cash-out paths <- registrar/host and financial-institution enforcement decisions <- agency request or seizure warrant <- exact legal basis, case scope, and custody evidence. The report does not identify a specific victim or prove a particular provider ignored a valid notice.

## Authority-forward trace
USSS/DOJ/FTC gather evidence and issue lawful notices or obtain court orders -> registrar/host/bank/exchange independently evaluates and executes permitted suspension/freeze -> dated technical or financial receipt -> downstream availability and funds custody -> replacement detection, appeal/correction for false positives, legal forfeiture and restitution administration -> realized human consequence. Regulatory or investigative authority does not automatically grant direct control of private infrastructure or payment rails.

## Rival explanations, contrary evidence, and omissions
- Many of the 5,800 requests may have succeeded; missing compliance figures **do not prove** provider inaction. Some requests may be duplicates, revised, contested or not within a single campaign; the source does not specify the unit of count.
- Lawful preservation or forfeiture procedures can delay payments while protecting due process and competing claims; delay is not necessarily failure. Successful recoveries in other fraud cases are real but not evidence of scam-compound-specific restitution.
- Some independent providers may already maintain stronger prevention and appeals; the report is not a complete audit of them.
- A centralized reporting portal, proposed in the ReportScams.gov Act, could improve routing but is **not** proof of victim remedy, provider denial or operational availability; proposal is not enactment.
- No verified individual attribution, complete infrastructure inventory, per-domain notices/receipt sample, account-reentry series, payment distribution ledger, or legal finality data were retrieved.
- The JEC report is a **Minority report**, not a unanimous congressional finding or a court judgment. Its quoted agency statements are attributable but not independently replicated here.
