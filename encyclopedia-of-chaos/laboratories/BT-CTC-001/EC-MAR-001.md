# EC-MAR-001 | The Emergency Control Nobody Could Use
Candidate v0.1 | YELLOW | Original domain: maritime engineering.
Source: ATSB MO-2025-009, Matthew Flinders III, incident 23 July 2025, report 7 July 2026. Reopened 24 November 2026: https://www.atsb.gov.au/investigations/mo-2025-009 . Public source pointer only; no custody-grade snapshot or independent reproduction.

Original proposition / rejected assumption: availability of alternative steering implies operational recoverability. Challenged in this incident, not universally defeated for every design.
Failure conditions: linkage loosened, shared feedback lost, normal manual and automatic modes affected according to detailed narrative; alternative control unfamiliar to crew. Procedures, labels and drills inadequate according to ATSB.
Observed consequences (investigator-reported): loss of normal steering, limited heading control obtained by differential propulsion, normal steering restored after repair.
Surviving useful information: separate non-follow-up control and independent indicator feedback, alternative propulsion, operational training and mode-transfer procedure. Counterevidence: alternative control remained available; not a total loss of all controllability.
Uncertainty: conflicting wording in source; cross-domain generalization and exact physical/digital equivalence unestablished.
