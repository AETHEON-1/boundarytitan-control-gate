## Dependency inquiry for each decisive observation

Record the observation ID and exact claim; original source/version/digest and capture time; transformations and excluded records; observed event time, commit order and receipt time; generation, selection, alteration, suppression and recovery control; actual owner/custodian/admin and permission scope; shared code, dataset, policy, specification, oracle, network and identity dependencies; reviewer conflicts and unresolved dependencies; competing explanation; separately held contradictory evidence or counterexample. If a party can change several channels, identify that common control dimension. UNKNOWN is valid. Do not assert that separation in one dimension proves statistical independence or independence in every dimension. Do not count roots without defining the claim and relevant dependency relation.

Existing public question: Recursion Inversion investigates who can control a decisive observation and keeps external evaluation fallible. Apply this as a challenge, not as proof of an independence criterion. Test whether common dependencies are material to the particular claim; genuinely independent reviewers can still share a true premise, and shared source does not make all findings false.

## Conditional comparison

- Common-source agreement may reproduce an error, or correctly corroborate a narrow claim. Distinguish both.
- Separately controlled channels may compensate, but may also inherit common policy, sensor or custody failures. An externally held bad measurement is still bad evidence.
- A claim of independence is contradicted in its stated dimension by a demonstrated shared controlling dependency capable of altering the decisive evidence. An uninspected dependency makes that dimension unresolved, not automatically compromised.
- Separate evidence independence, enforcement independence and legitimate authority. No observation, reviewer identity or acceptance produces normative permission by itself.
