## Arrow map
Claim / research authorization → identity or credential → accessible interface → agent/tool action → downstream consequence → detection → attempted revocation → independently observed prevention.

First unsupported transition varies by case:
- Research authorization does not imply permission to target third-party systems.
- Credential acceptance does not imply legitimate cross-tenant authority.
- Sandbox labeling does not establish complete containment.
- Evaluation output does not itself establish binding release denial.
- Incident counts do not establish global prevalence.

## Control gap hypothesis, not certified failure
Test whether independent authorization checks dominate all declared consequential execution paths and whether revocation prevents residual queued/retried effects. A correct graph traversal does not prove that the declared graph is complete.

## Competing explanations / negative space
Human misuse, misconfiguration, compromised credentials, exposed endpoints, omitted enforcement layers, inaccurate reporting, incomplete source retrieval and alternate agent behaviors must be considered separately. No universal agent-escape mechanism is inferred.

## Falsifiers and independent review
1. Original incident record contradicts the described access, scope, timeline or causal chain.
2. A separately verified control actually denied the alleged forbidden consequence in the declared case.
3. Reported effect was only attempted, not executed.
4. Reproduced owned-fixture test shows the proposed mechanism is unreachable under pinned conditions.
5. An independently observed surviving post-revocation effect contradicts a claimed complete recovery.
6. A seeded omitted path defeats claimed exhaustive graph coverage.
