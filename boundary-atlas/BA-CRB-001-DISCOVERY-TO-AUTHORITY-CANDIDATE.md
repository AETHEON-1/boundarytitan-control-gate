## Consequence-first authority graph
Real-world source -> Retrieval instrument -> Search/discovery -> Observed results -> Interpretation -> Claim of sufficient coverage -> Institutional decision -> Real-world consequence.

## First unsupported arrow
Observed results -> Sufficient coverage.
A search result can accurately describe the returned items while omitting relevant source items. The absence of observed items does not prove absence of items in the declared universe.

## Second unsupported arrow
Sufficient evidence -> Legitimate authority to act.
Even correctly and completely collected evidence does not itself authorize a consequential action. Legitimacy, jurisdiction, owner, and enforceable control require independent grounding.

## Failure and challenge
Known-fixture retrieval reportedly succeeds by direct ID while discovery misses A and C and conditionally finds B. This is a candidate demonstration of method-dependent visibility, not independently verified proof of source coverage or a universal failure.
An independent specialist must challenge the source capture, raw evidence, search coverage, alternative explanations, and the authority transition.
The external stop must prevent unsupported consequential action, not merely issue a warning or record an objection. Stop-path effectiveness is NOT TESTED in this case.

Each domain requires its own standards, competent reviewers, legitimate decision authority, and tested brake; analogies do not establish equivalence.
