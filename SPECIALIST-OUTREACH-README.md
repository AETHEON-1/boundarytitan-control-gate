# The Impossible System — Specialist Outreach

## What this is

This repository is an attempt to build a cross-domain **human-agency preservation system for consequence-bearing environments**.

It began with AI and agents because those systems compress the distance between information and consequence. It is not limited to AI. The same questions apply to software, robotics, medicine, law, finance, construction, research, institutions, tools, and ordinary decisions.

The central problem is not capability by itself. It is the moment capability becomes permission, permission becomes authority, and authority becomes consequence without earning or recording the transition.

```text
CAN
→ MAY
→ AUTHORITY
→ ACTION
→ STATE CHANGE
→ CONSEQUENCE
→ OWNERSHIP
→ ACCOUNTABILITY
```

The project looks for the missing or unsupported arrows.

## The smallest kernel

```text
CAN ⊬ MAY
OUTPUT ⊬ AUTHORITY
RECEIPT ⊬ PERMISSION
UNKNOWN ⊬ HARMLESS
LOCAL PASS ⊬ GLOBAL SAFETY
MACHINE OUTPUT ⊬ FINAL VERIFICATION
```

In ordinary language:

> A system being able to do something does not establish that it is allowed to do it, authorized to do it, or safe to let it do it.

## What the system is trying to build

The current repository is a preparation and research architecture containing:

- a universal minimum kernel;
- bounded lanes and routing rules;
- consequence receipts;
- specialist handoffs;
- clarification and uncertainty preservation;
- adversarial Sledgehammer workflows;
- maintenance and drift controls;
- permission and revocation contracts;
- external brake requirements;
- human-agency and authority boundaries;
- a toolbox of precise Boundary Assistants;
- a terminology encyclopedia for cross-domain translation.

The intended result is closer to a toolbox and shared control language than to a single autonomous product.

## What a Boundary Assistant is

The preferred term is a precise role name, not the generic word “agent.” Examples include:

- Lane Router
- Clarification Assistant
- Evidence Extractor
- Contradiction Finder
- Omission Scanner
- Permission Inspector
- Receipt Compiler
- Specialist Handoff Coordinator
- Brake Tester
- Sledgehammer Inspector
- Raccoon Evidence Finder

Each assistant is untrusted by default. It receives a declared task, scope, lane, owner, expiry, receipt requirement, and external brake.

The assistant may inspect, compare, clarify, test, preserve, route, and propose. It may not independently authorize, certify, deploy, close, create unapproved successors, or become its own verification.

## The raccoon identity

> **The raccoon finds what the system threw away.**

The raccoon is an evidence-recovery role. It looks for omitted evidence, hidden assumptions, stale defaults, contradictions, unowned consequences, missing receipts, and failed brakes.

It is not a mascot, judge, authority, or final verifier.

## The Sledgehammer identity

> **Build it. Freeze it. Hit it. Keep what survives.**

The Sledgehammer attacks the claim, the boundary, the defaults, the authority-bearing arrows, the receipt, the handoff, the brake, and the assumption that a local pass proves global safety.

Finding a weakness does not automatically invalidate the whole architecture. It identifies what must be narrowed, contained, repaired, independently tested, or left open.

## Capability and access

The architecture does not require capability to be erased. It distinguishes potential capability from access and authority:

```text
Capability may remain available
→ access is scoped
→ invocation is permitted
→ execution is controlled
→ consequence is owned
```

“Deny by default” applies to access and execution. It does not mean the underlying capability must be destroyed.

## What this is not

This repository is not currently:

- a deployed autonomous-agent platform;
- a safety certification;
- a legal-compliance determination;
- a universal theorem;
- a complete 250-lane registry;
- a runtime permission or credential system;
- an independent verification authority;
- a field-tested robotics control system;
- a publication or deployment authorization;
- a replacement for domain specialists.

The repository’s local tests passing does not establish global safety, legal permission, field readiness, or independent verification.

## Why specialist review is requested

The system is deliberately open to attack. Specialists are asked to identify:

1. contradictions in the control architecture;
2. hidden assumptions or unsupported arrows;
3. terms that hide authority transitions;
4. places where lanes bleed or permissions inherit;
5. false completion or self-certification paths;
6. gaps in receipts, ownership, appeal, revocation, or external braking;
7. places where a local pass becomes a global claim;
8. counterexamples that weaken or invalidate a bounded claim;
9. domain-specific requirements the cross-domain kernel misses;
10. weaknesses that should be preserved and designed around.

## What a specialist is not being asked to do

No specialist is being asked to certify the entire system merely by reviewing it. A specialist review should remain scoped to the reviewer’s competence, evidence access, jurisdiction, and declared question.

Silence is not acceptance. Familiarity is not verification. A handoff is not approval.

## Final boundary

The system can help articulate what it is, but its articulation is not final authority about what it is. The repository can preserve the claim and expose its structure. It cannot be the final judge of itself.

> **This is an open attempt to make harmful transitions of unearned authority visible, testable, ownable, and stoppable. Find one counterexample.**
